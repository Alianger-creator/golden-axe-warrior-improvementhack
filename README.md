​I've put together some improvement hacks for Golden Axe Warrior (SMS), a pretty solid Zelda-like plagued by a few problems such as the grinding, sluggish early combat and some frustrating enemy encounters, and being pretty linear compared to Zelda 1 and 3. These new hacks aim to make the adventure smoother and a bit more open-ended without taking away the exploration and resource management that make the game rewarding to play. 
In the QoL hack, enemy knockback is extended, a more complex drop rate system makes enemies drop useful items in a way that's adapted to your current situation, early game exploration is a tad easier, dungeon crystals send you straight outside after beating a boss, and the Magic Feather can now be used inside labyrinths. There are also a new shortcut at one point, a more flexible progression through the game, better magic recovery and recovery after death. Finally, I've added Mega Drive controller support with convenient item cycling!

The Balance Hack gives selected enemies and bosses somewhat altered movement and attack patterns, closes off boss-room exploits, and makes the later dungeons more dangerous. 
The Graphics Tweaks Hack fixes a few minor visual inconsistencies and makes magic scrolls visually distinct depending on their level, while the Music Hack adds an audio clue to the 14 caves where your horns can get stolen. I was also planning to add a new final boss theme, but sadly there wasn't enough space for it. 


Quality of Life Hack v1.0:
Enemies now drop items more consistently, with increased horn drops and better rewards from several tougher enemies. Magic pots are replaced when your magic is full, and enemies give you other useful items instead of wasting drops when your wallet is full. Enemies like chest mimics, wizards and clamshell monsters have improved drop tables. Some enemies also have an increased chance of dropping blue horns, while eyeball enemies provide more magic pots to reduce unnecessary grinding and backtracking. Drop rates adapt to your situation, increasing the chances of receiving food when your health is low or magic pots when your magic is running low. The system is also adjusted to avoid making the game too generous when your resources are already plentiful.
Enemies that can be knocked back are pushed farther away when hit, making close range combat less of a constant back and forth struggle. Green goblins/snoutmen also take two hits to defeat instead of three. More players can now explore more of the early game freely rather than going straight for the extra heart container and first dungeon. 
The Magic Feather can now be used inside labyrinths to return to their entrance, rather than forcing you to walk all the way back or deliberately lose a life. The feather can also be selected normally while inside a dungeon.
Collecting a dungeon's crystal after defeating its boss now automatically takes you outside, eliminating unnecessary backtracking.
The torch lights dark rooms automatically once you own it, without changing your currently selected item. No more need to open the menu and select it over and over in certain dungeons. 
Dropped items remain on screen twice as long, giving you more time to collect food, horns and other items during or after a fight.
Some progression requirements have been relaxed, allowing earlier access to certain equipment and magic upgrades. The deep water near the swamp dungeon has also been changed to open up additional routes through the eastern part of the world (replacing the arbitrary appearance of a bridge after beating this dungeon).
A new shortcut in the southwestern forest removes an annoying dead end and makes travelling towards the Dragon Mail and the nearby town more convenient. A hidden late game cave can also be revealed by chopping down its tree with the axe, although the Ice Bell still works (this is more intuitive considering the lack of a clue about using the latter and it normally being used on lava and rocks).
Mega Drive controller support adds more convenient item selection. You can cycle through owned items while moving, using the A button for attacking or using items, B to cycle the next items, holding A and pressing B for the previous items, C for the menu/map and Start for pausing. The original Master System controls remain unchanged. 
Magic Oil costs half as much, and Earth Magic's effective MP cost is reduced, letting you experiment more freely during exploration without having to backtrack for a shop or inn as often. Inns now restore up to five pots of magic in addition to health.
Continuing after death now restores approximately two-thirds of your maximum health and magic, rather than a fixed three hearts and three pots (which is still the minimum). You still lose 1/4 of your money each time. This makes carrying on with whatever you were up to something to consider later in the game, rather than having to always backtrack to a shop or inn later in the game, while maintaining some punishment for death. 

Balance Hack v1.0:
The original game has some uneven enemy encounters, with certain enemies being frustrating to hit while others are too passive. This hack adjusts selected enemies and bosses to make combat more engaging, while reducing repetitive or overly exploitable encounters.
Boss rooms no longer allow you to stand safely in the doorway or simply walk out while the boss is alive. Mini-boss rooms are unaffected, and the Magic Feather still provides a way to escape.
Bats land somewhat more frequently, giving you better opportunities to hit them instead of chasing their erratic flight patterns.
Green skeleton swordsmen and green demons move about 18.5% faster, making them a bit more active opponents.
Green flames move around a bit more often instead of spending so much time sitting still.
Desert whirlwinds stop and spin somewhat more frequently, creating more opportunities to attack them.
The dragons in Dungeons 2 and 6, along with the red dragon mini-boss in the ice dungeon, have revised attack patterns. Their fireballs have some random sideways drift, and their attacks have longer wind-ups and cooldowns, making them less predictable without simply turning them into damage sponges. This also removes the easily found safespot when fighting them. 
The Dungeon 3 boss's ball form now has a six-second limit followed by a two-second cooldown, preventing it from remaining in ball form for too long or immediately repeating the attack. It also affects the later mini-boss. 
The water dungeon's teleporting mage boss becomes slightly faster each time you damage it: its wind-up and time spent invisible both decrease with each hit, making it a somewhat more intense fight.
Teleporting eyeball enemies remain fully visible for a bit longer after firing, giving you more time to attack them in melee.
The Dungeon 6 dragon deals a bit more damage when touched, as does its five-way fan attack, and spike traps become blue and deal a full heart of damage from Dungeon 6 onwards. These were trivial in the original

Graphics Tweaks Hack v1.0:
A small collection of graphical fixes and improvements to make the presentation more consistent and polish a few details.
Corrects the inverted highlights on the blue horns and the short and long swords in the inventory by swapping their grey and cyan shades.
Fixes the ending's scrolling text passing behind the axe and crystals. The axe and crystal sprites now fade out of view before the text scrolls on screen, then return afterwards, allowing the text to remain readable without losing the original presentation.
Adds distinct level 2 icons for the blue, green and red magic scrolls, making it easier to see which magic upgrades you have acquired.

Music Hack v1.0:
Adds the Thief's Theme from Golden Axe to the 14 caves where the gnome or dwarf steals your horns, as a clue. The new music is converted to the game's own sound format and integrated into the existing music system, while other caves and the overworld retain their original music.
